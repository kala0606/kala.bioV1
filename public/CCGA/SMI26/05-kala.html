KALA

Your instructor, briefly. KALA is the name Ujjwal Agarwal works under as a generative artist. The word means art in Hindi and time in Sanskrit; he means both.

Computer engineering at MNIT Jaipur; an MA in Computational Arts at Goldsmiths, University of London; then a decade of installations, sound, and real-time work — a clock tower in Jodhpur turned into a timeline, a flying machine, rooms that listen. He has taught at SMI since 2024. He describes the practice as one "where algorithm, chance and the present moment become something you can feel," and organises it under a made-up belief system, the Order of Kala, which reveres Time as the ultimate power and truth. Take that as seriously as you like.

The part that belongs in this history is small and specific: three long-form generative series, on the two platforms from chapter 4.

JAN 2023

KARMA — Art Blocks Presents

33 editions. A flock. Craig Reynolds's boids, in the version Daniel Shiffman teaches, with colour tricks learned from Joshua Davis. The work is interactive: click and hold anywhere and the flock draws; press the down arrow and the canvas clears. The statement calls karma "the original algorithm of Indian thought. Action, inaction, randomness, will — all are ground in its maw to determine the manifest shape of the next moment." Which is also, exactly, a description of a flocking simulation.

FEB 2023

The Yoni Project — fx(hash)

800 editions, on Tezos. An homage to Shakti, the divine feminine — forms grown by code around the oldest symbol there is. Shown at the India Art Fair, New Delhi, 9–12 February 2023, with Tezos India: four artists, four eight-foot kiosks, 3,200 generative works given away free to visitors who set up a wallet on the spot. For many of them it was the first generative artwork they had ever owned, and it cost nothing. Compare chapter 4.

2024 – 2025

Raga.fm and Songs of Utopia — Art Blocks Studio

Raga.fm is a generative raga engine. Each token draws one of 45 ragas from its hash and performs it live in the browser — the permitted notes, the ascending and descending paths, the characteristic phrases, the time of day — through a sampled ensemble in Tone.js, with a p5 visual field that moves with the music. Correct to tradition; never the same twice; authored by no single hand. It runs at raga.fm. Songs of Utopia (200 editions, January 2025) continues the idea: "an infinite composition of music in time, each unique and each personal."

What KARMA is made of

Below is the flock. Three rules per bird per frame — move away from anyone too close, steer towards the average heading of your neighbours, drift toward their centre — and nothing else. There is no picture in the code; only the trail of the decisions. Hold the mouse down to pull them. Press any key to clear.

Try: SIGHT = 200 — one flock. SIGHT = 20 — none. Remove the ali line: they gather but never agree. Then decide which of the three rules is "karma".

Where this leaves you

Sunflower, kolam, LeWitt, Molnár, Mohr, Snowfro, KALA. Different centuries, one move: write the rule, let it run, look hard at what comes out, change the rule. From next week you will do exactly that, in this room, with p5.js. The history is finished. Yours is not.
