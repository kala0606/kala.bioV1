Art Blocks & the money

For fifty years generative art was plotter drawings in flat files that almost nobody bought. Then, in about eighteen months, it became the most expensive art being made by living artists. The numbers below are real, and they move — because the currency they were paid in has never stopped moving.

First, the currency

Almost everything on this page was paid in ether (ETH), the currency of the Ethereum blockchain. Its price against the dollar and the rupee changes every second. Every figure below is therefore two figures: what it was worth on the day, and what the same number of ETH is worth right now.

The timeline, with receipts

  • JUNE 2017CryptoPunks

    Matt Hall and John Watkinson of Larva Labs generate 10,000 pixel-art faces and put them on Ethereum. Free to claim; you paid only the transaction fee. Most went unclaimed for days. In 2021 a single Punk sold for $11.8M. The Centre Pompidou acquired one in 2023.

  • APRIL 2019Autoglyphs

    Larva Labs again. This time the program lives on the blockchain; the chain draws the picture. 512 outputs for a creation fee of 0.2 ETH (about $35), donated to a climate charity, then the generator shut itself off. Generative art was now on-chain, and scarce by construction.

  • 27 NOV 2020Art Blocks launches — Chromie Squiggle #0

    Erick Calderon — Snowfro — a tile importer in Houston who had been making generative art in Processing for years, builds a platform with one rule: the artist uploads a program, the collector pays to mint, and only then does the program run, seeded by the transaction hash. Nobody, including the artist, knows what they will get. Project #0 is his own Chromie Squiggle: one line, walking a noise field, coloured along the spectrum. 10,000 planned; 9,000 released; the rest kept in reserve. Early mints cost a few dollars' worth of ETH.

  • JAN 2021Ringers, Archetype

    Dmitri Cherniak's Ringers: "an almost infinite number of ways to wrap a string around a set of pegs." Kjetil Golid's Archetype. Both 1,000 editions. The Art Blocks "Curated" tier begins to mean something.

  • 11 MAR 2021Beeple at Christie's

    Mike Winkelmann had posted one image a day for 13½ years. Christie's sells the collage of all 5,000 as an NFT for $69,346,250 — 42,329 ETH, to Vignesh Sundaresan, a Singapore-based crypto investor from Tamil Nadu, who bid as "MetaKovan". It is the third-highest price ever paid for a living artist. Not generative art — but it is the day the world learned what an NFT was. The same 42,329 ETH today: —.

  • 22 MAR 2021The first tweet

    Jack Dorsey's "just setting up my twttr" (21 Mar 2006), sold as an NFT for 1,630.58 ETH ≈ $2.9M to Sina Estavi, a Malaysia-based crypto executive. Dorsey gave the money to charity. Hold this thought.

  • 11 JUN 2021Fidenza

    Tyler Hobbs, an Austin painter-programmer, releases 999 flow-field compositions at 0.17 ETH (~$400). Sold out in about 25 minutes. Within two months, single Fidenzas were trading for the price of a house. The mint price, in today's ETH: —.

  • AUG – OCT 2021The summer

    Fidenza #313: 1,000 ETH ($3.3M), bought at mint for $1,400. Ringers #879, "The Goose", to the hedge fund Three Arrows Capital: 1,800 ETH ($5.66M). Chromie Squiggle #7583: 945 ETH ($2.89M). Ringers #109: 2,100 ETH ($6.93M), still the highest Art Blocks sale. Art Blocks does $294M of trading in August 2021 alone; by year-end, over $1.1 billion. Sotheby's runs its first generative-art sale. Matt DesLauriers's Meridian, Emily Xie, Zancan, William Mapan, Casey Reas himself.

  • NOV 2021fx(hash) — and the peak

    On the Tezos chain, where a transaction costs a fraction of a cent, the artist ciphrd launches fx(hash): no curation, no application, anyone can publish. It becomes the place for experiment, for artists outside the US and Europe, and for editions of 800 at a few tez each. On 10 Nov, ETH hits $4,878. Nobody knows it is the top.

  • APR 2022The tweet, again

    Sina Estavi lists the Dorsey tweet NFT for $48 million, promising half to charity. The week-long auction closes with seven bids. The highest is 0.09 ETH — about $280. The NFT has since been passed around and mocked; it has never sold again. Nothing about the tweet changed. Everything about the room did.

  • MAY – NOV 2022The crash

    Terra/Luna collapses in May. Three Arrows Capital — owner of The Goose — is bankrupt by June. ETH falls to about $1,000. FTX collapses in November. NFT trading volume falls by more than 90%. Most projects go to zero. The good ones do not.

  • JAN – FEB 2023KALA

    KARMA, 33 editions, on Art Blocks. The Yoni Project, 800 editions on fx(hash), shown on eight-foot kiosks at the India Art Fair with Tezos India. See chapter 5.

  • 15 JUN 2023The Goose, again

    Sotheby's auctions the bankrupt hedge fund's collection as Grails. The Goose sells for $6.2M — a gain in dollars over the $5.66M paid, and since ETH was at ~$1,750, about — in ETH terms at today's price. Which number is the "real" one? Also in 2023: LACMA accepts 22 works including Squiggles as a gift; the Pompidou acquires a CryptoPunk and an Autoglyph; Vera Molnár, at 99, releases a generative series through Sotheby's.

  • 2024 – 2026Now

    Art Blocks reorganises into Curated, Presents, and artist-run Studio contracts. fx(hash) adds Ethereum and Base. Volumes are a fraction of 2021; the artists who were serious in 2019 are still working. ETH makes a new high of $4,953 in August 2025, falls below $1,700 by mid-2026, and is wherever the ticker at the top says it is.

What the numbers are for

Not for envy, and not for cynicism — both are cheap. Three things worth taking from them:

  • Value is a system too. A Fidenza did not change between June and August 2021. The room did. Art has always worked this way — Van Gogh sold one painting — but the blockchain made the ledger public, so for the first time everyone could watch the system run.
  • The hash is a formal idea, not a financial one. Seed a program with a number nobody chose, and the output is honest in a way a hand-picked "best of 1,000" is not. That is the idea Art Blocks borrowed from Molnár and LeWitt. It works whether or not anyone pays for it.
  • The tweet is the lesson. $2.9M, then $280. Same object, same chain, same owner. If your work only means what it last sold for, it means nothing. Make the thing that would still be worth making at $280.

Try: STEPS = 40 — the rare "hyper-rainbow" trait. Then draw circles instead of lines: replace the line(...) with circle(x, y, 14) and see the "dots" trait appear. Snowfro's features are just branches in the code.

Try: strokeWeight(s * 0.8) — the string becomes the picture. Then keep only 3 pegs. Cherniak's real algorithm wraps the string tangentially around each peg; this one cheats with a curve. Can you see the difference?
