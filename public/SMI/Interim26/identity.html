Before you begin

An authored identity is a position from which to make work about a real condition. You will speak from it for a month, and people who live inside that condition may stand in front of the work.

  • It answers to the world. Start from a condition you can point to with evidence, not from a character you would like to play.
  • It is not a costume. No mascots, puns or jokes, and no escape from the present. Displacement is a way to see a real situation more clearly.
  • It is not you under another name. Its distance from you is the point. You will write down where you differ.

Work through Parts A–F alone in the morning, do the interview in G after lunch, then H with Codex. Your answers save in this browser as you type. When you're done, use Copy as identity.md at the bottom of the page and paste the result into your repo, or print the page and write by hand.

A

The condition

Bring forward the condition from Day 2 behind the candidate you are keeping.

B

The displacement

The identity stands somewhere other than where you stand. Mark the coordinate that has moved: one, or at most two.

C

The facts

Facts make the identity specific enough to question, and to disagree with.

D

Attention

A position is defined as much by what it turns away from as by what it looks at.

E

Beliefs and rules

Beliefs are what the identity holds to be true. Rules are what its work must therefore do. In Week 2 the rules become constraints in code.

F

The distance

Be honest about the gap between you and the identity. You'll close part of it with research tomorrow.

G

Pair interview

Twenty minutes each way. Your partner asks; you answer in the first person, as the identity. Don't break position to explain.

  1. Walk me through yesterday, hour by hour.
  2. Where does your work go when it is finished, and who sees it?
  3. Who would disagree with you, and what would they say?
  4. What has working this way cost you?
  5. What would you never make, even if you were paid to?
  6. What do you need the world to change?

Your partner fills in the next two fields, in their own words.

H

Codex as interlocutor

Ask Codex for computational metaphors, not code. Paste your Parts A, B and E into the prompt where marked.

I am developing an authored identity for a generative art studio. It speaks from a real condition and a specific position. Here are its condition, position, beliefs and rules:

[paste Parts A, B and E]

Don't write any code. Propose five computational metaphors for how this identity might make work. For each one, give:
- the process (for example erosion, a survey grid, recursion, a flow field, an agent that forages),
- what in the condition it stands for,
- one way it could fail to carry the identity's position.

Then ask me two questions about the identity that would sharpen these metaphors.

A worked example

An abridged sheet, to show the level of specificity expected. Don't borrow its condition.

Anjana Rao, surveyor of tank beds

Example
A · Condition
The loss of Bengaluru's irrigation tanks to layouts and roads since the 1970s, and the groundwater decline that followed.
A · Evidence
Lake encroachment reports; old Survey of India sheets of the city; borewell depth records for the eastern wards.
B · Displacement
Role and time. A government land surveyor in 2041, still sent to measure tanks that exist only in the records.
B · What it sees
That the city's maps record what it owns, never what it has lost.
C · Origin
Born 1999 in a village whose tank was built over in 2004. Trained as a surveyor because her father kept the old tank's boundary stones.
D · Refuses
Aerial images of the city as beautiful pattern. She will not make a picture of loss that is pleasant to look at.
E · Belief
A boundary is a promise that was broken, not a line.
E · Rule
Because only measurement counts as proof, the work must draw nothing that has not been measured, and must draw absence heavier than presence.
H · Kept metaphor
The survey grid as a sieve: each cell keeps the water that should be there, and the drawing records the deficit.
