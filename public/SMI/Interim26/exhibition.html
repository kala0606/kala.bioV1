5

QR code

To the repository, where the system lives.

  • Height. Hang work with its centre about 145–150 cm from the floor. Labels and the QR code sit beside the work, around 130–140 cm.
  • Order. Decide what a visitor meets first. Usually the artefact draws them in, and the card and manifesto tell them who is speaking.
  • Room to stand. Leave space in front of the work for two or three people, and clear sight of the label.

The statement

Day 16. 100–150 words for visitors, in final-statement.md. Where the manifesto is the identity speaking, the statement is you, introducing the work.

Manifesto

The identity speaks

First person, as the identity. A position, refusals, rules.

Statement

You introduce the work

Third person about the identity. What a visitor is looking at, how it was made, why.

final-statement.md
# [Title of the work]

[Identity name] is [practice], [one phrase on the position and the condition].

[What the visitor is looking at: the object, its material, its scale.]

[How it was made: the system's key rule in plain words, and the data or input it reads.]

[Why: what the work asks the visitor to see or reconsider.]

[Your name], 2026. Made in Computationally Yours: Out of One's Place.

Deficit Survey, Tank No. 14

Example

Anjana Rao is a surveyor of tank beds, measuring the irrigation tanks Bengaluru has built over since the 1970s.

This A2 pen plot is a survey of one lost tank. Each row of lines is a year of rainfall from 1990 to 2024; the heavier the line, the greater that year's deficit. Nothing is drawn outside the tank's recorded boundary.

The plotter drew every line from a system that reads the rainfall record and obeys three rules from Rao's manifesto. Its heaviest passes put the most ink where the most water is missing.

The work asks what a map of the city would look like if it recorded loss as carefully as ownership.

About 110 words

Error correction level M, with a four-module quiet border.

Use the SVG for print: it stays sharp at any size. Prefer to make it with code? Ask Codex: "Make an SVG QR code for my press link with error correction M and a quiet zone of four modules."

Waiting for an address.

06

Print it

Size

At least 3 cm

Square, for scanning from about arm's length. Bigger if visitors will stand further back.

Contrast

Black on white

Dark modules on a light ground. No colour inversions, gradients or logos in the middle.

Border

Keep the margin

Don't crop the white border around the code; scanners need it.

Print on matte paper or card: gloss reflects the exhibition lights straight into the phone's camera.

The Collective Atlas

The atlas places all twenty identities on one shared map: where each one stands, and which conditions connect them.

  1. Day 16 · your coordinates.

    Each identity is given a position on the atlas from its displacement: the place, time, language, role or scale it speaks from.

  2. Day 18 · your entry.

    Submit the entry below. Name two other identities whose conditions touch yours.

  3. Day 19 · assembly.

    The atlas is assembled together, as a wall, a table or a plotted map, and the connections are drawn.

10

Floor plan

Day 18. The gallery layout is fixed on the floor plan together, so neighbouring zones talk to each other.

  • Group by connection, not by medium. Identities that share a condition can sit near each other, the way the atlas links them.
  • Mind the machines' needs. Resin objects need plinths and light; prints and plots need wall; anything with a screen needs power.
  • Leave a route. A visitor should be able to walk the whole atlas without doubling back.

13

Sixty seconds

You'll say this many times on opening day. Write it, time it, and then say it without the paper.

Sixty-second introduction
1 · Who: "This is [identity], a [practice] who [position, in one line]."  (10 s)
2 · Why: "[The condition], and why it matters now."                        (15 s)
3 · How: "The work is made by a system that [the key rule, plainly]."       (15 s)
4 · What: "What you're looking at is [the object], and [what to notice]."  (15 s)
5 · Invitation: "The code and the whole process are behind the QR code."   (5 s)

Speak as the author, not in costume. Visitors want to know who is speaking and why it matters; let the identity's own voice stay in the manifesto.
