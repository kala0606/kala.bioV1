01

The show

The exhibition reads as one archive, not twenty separate projects: a set of positions on the world you're about to enter, each placed in relation to the others.

It opens on the evening of Day 20, Friday 18 December, and stays open on Saturday 19 December, in the studio's usual exhibition space. Take-down times will be confirmed. To be confirmed

02

The zone

Each identity has one zone. Every zone holds the same five things, so a visitor learns to read them once and can then move through the whole atlas.

1

Identity card

Who is speaking, from where.

2

Manifesto

150 words, printed large enough to read standing.

3

The artefact

Plot, print, resin, or a combination.

4

Label

Title, medium, dimensions, seed, and your name.

5

QR code

To the repository, where the system lives.

  • Height. Hang work with its centre about 145–150 cm from the floor. Labels and the QR code sit beside the work, around 130–140 cm.
  • Order. Decide what a visitor meets first. Usually the artefact draws them in, and the card and manifesto tell them who is speaking.
  • Room to stand. Leave space in front of the work for two or three people, and clear sight of the label.
03

The Collective Atlas

The atlas places all twenty identities on one shared map: where each one stands, and which conditions connect them.

  1. Day 16 · your coordinates.

    Each identity is given a position on the atlas from its displacement: the place, time, language, role or scale it speaks from.

  2. Day 18 · your entry.

    Submit the entry below. Name two other identities whose conditions touch yours.

  3. Day 19 · assembly.

    The atlas is assembled together, as a wall, a table or a plotted map, and the connections are drawn.

Atlas entry · Day 18
Identity: [name]
Coordinates: [the coordinate that moved] · [where exactly it stands]
Condition: [in one line]

Connected to: [identity] because [shared condition]
              [identity] because [shared condition]

05

Floor plan

Day 18. The gallery layout is fixed on the floor plan together, so neighbouring zones talk to each other.

  • Group by connection, not by medium. Identities that share a condition can sit near each other, the way the atlas links them.
  • Mind the machines' needs. Resin objects need plinths and light; prints and plots need wall; anything with a screen needs power.
  • Leave a route. A visitor should be able to walk the whole atlas without doubling back.
