  • Submitting means pushing. Each submission is the state of your repository at the deadline, plus what you present in the room.
  • Each submission builds on the last. You can keep improving the identity and the system after A1 and A2; A3 assesses where they end up, through the artefact and the exhibition.

—

Requirements

By A3, every submission must meet these before the rubric is applied.

  • One authored identity, with a manifesto of 150 words or fewer.
  • An original generative system that you can explain. AI image generation on its own is not a system.
  • At least one exhibition-quality physical artefact: a plot, a resin object, a print, or any combination.
  • A public repository with the files in the submission checklists.
  • A daily log with an entry for most studio days, and an authorship log.

How critiques run

A critique takes the whole day. Each of you presents for three minutes, then takes questions, with your peer group in the room; everyone else keeps working. Three things, in this order:

  1. Who is speaking.

    The identity, its condition and its position, in two sentences. Don't read the manifesto unless it's Critique 1.

  2. What's on the wall.

    Point to the work. Say what it is and how it was made.

  3. Your question.

    The one thing you most need the room to think about. "Is it good?" doesn't count.

Pin up before your slot: the work, plus that week's log on one A4 sheet so reviewers can read it while you set up.

—

Peer notes

In the last two minutes, each person in the room writes three lines and hands them over.

Notes card
For: [identity name] · Critique [1/2/3] · From: [your name]

What holds: [the strongest thing, specifically]
What's thin: [where the position or the work goes vague]
A question: [one you couldn't ask in the time]

Write about the work and the position, not the person. Be specific enough that the note is useful tomorrow morning.

After the critique

  1. Photograph the notes cards.

    Into log/, the same day.

  2. Write that day's log entry about the critique.

    Decided: the one change you'll make first, and why the identity would make it.

  3. Start Monday with it.

    The next week's first day begins from the critique, not from where you were before it.

Integrity

  • Credit everything you used: data, references, code you adapted, and help from people.
  • Codex is a tool you declare, through the authorship log. Using it is expected; hiding what it did is not.
  • Words are yours. A manifesto, statement or log written by an AI is not your work.
