Three submissions

  • Submitting means pushing. Each submission is the state of your repository at the deadline, plus what you present in the room.
  • Each submission builds on the last. You can keep improving the identity and the system after A1 and A2; A3 assesses where they end up, through the artefact and the exhibition.
  • Critique 3 is feedback only. It falls inside A3, so it isn't marked; use it to get the artefact right.
—

Requirements

By A3, every submission must meet these before the rubric is applied.

  • One authored identity, with a manifesto of 150 words or fewer.
  • An original generative system that you can explain. AI image generation on its own is not a system.
  • At least one exhibition-quality physical artefact: a plot, a resin object, a print, or any combination.
  • A public repository with the files in the submission checklists.
  • A daily log with an entry for most studio days, and an authorship log.

A3

Material and exhibition — stand behind it

Due Day 20 · Fri 18 Dec, before the walkthrough · assessed in the exhibition
50%
Submit

The question

Does the physical work embody the system, and does it let a visitor follow the identity's position from rules to object?

During the walkthrough or straight after, a short conversation at your zone: walk through the system and answer questions about any part of it.

Integrity

  • Credit everything you used: data, references, code you adapted, and help from people.
  • Codex is a tool you declare, through the authorship log. Using it is expected; hiding what it did is not.
  • Words are yours. A manifesto, statement or log written by an AI is not your work.
