What is assessed

Three bodies of evidence, read together, and a short conversation.

The zone

In the exhibition

The artefact, the identity card, the manifesto, the statement and the QR code, as installed.

The repository

Behind the QR

The system's code and every document listed in the final submission checklist.

The record

How it was made

The daily log, the authorship log, the test log, and the three critiques' notes.

During the walkthrough on Day 20 or straight after, each student has a short conversation with the facilitator at their zone: walk through the system, and answer questions about any part of it.

—

Requirements

Every submission must meet these before the rubric is applied.

  • One authored identity, with a manifesto of 150 words or fewer.
  • An original generative system that you can explain. AI image generation on its own is not a system.
  • At least one exhibition-quality physical artefact: a plot, a resin object, a print, or any combination.
  • A public repository with the files in the final submission checklist.
  • A daily log with an entry for most studio days, and an authorship log.

The rubric

CriterionExceptionalAccomplishedDevelopingBeginning
Identity and position20% A distinctive position of real substance; the condition is precise, researched and felt throughout. A clear, serious position tied to a specific condition, held consistently. A position that is present but general, or wavers across the work. A theme or a character rather than a position; reads as costume.
Generative system25% Original rules that are the identity's argument; wide range in one unmistakable language; fully reproducible. Rules traceable to the manifesto, a recognisable language, reproducible from seeds. Works, but leans on a technique's defaults; limited range or reproducibility. Little beyond a tutorial or a single image; can't be reproduced or explained.
Artefact and material20% The material is part of the argument; resolved, exhibition-ready craft. A well-made object clearly produced by the system, in a fitting material. An object made, with problems of craft or a weak link to the system. Unfinished, or not recognisably the system's output.
Exhibition10% The zone reads as a whole, with a clear order and the atlas connection made visible. All elements present, well placed and legible. Elements present but crowded, unclear or untested. Elements missing; QR or labels don't work.
Process and documentation15% A complete, honest record; process.md would teach a stranger how the work is made. Log, tests, process and photographs all present and specific. Gaps in the log or thin documents written at the end. Little record of how the work was made.
Authorship and AI10% Explains every line; the authorship log shows sharp, reasoned decisions against the machine. Explains the system fully; clear record of what Codex did and what was rejected. Explains most of it; the record of decisions is thin. Can't explain the code, or the work is mostly the machine's.
—

Integrity

  • Credit everything you used: data, references, code you adapted, and help from people.
  • Codex is a tool you declare, through the authorship log. Using it is expected; hiding what it did is not.
  • Words are yours. A manifesto, statement or log written by an AI is not your work.
