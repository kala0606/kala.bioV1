01

The entry

depth 2

Four fields, every day, in this order.

Field 1

Did

What changed in the system today. Name the rule, the parameter or the file, not just "worked on code".

Field 2

Decided

One choice you made, and why your identity would make it. This is the most important line.

Field 3

Image

One output from today, saved as log/day-07.png. A photo of a test or a plot counts.

Field 4

Stuck

One open question to start tomorrow with. If nothing is stuck, write what you are least sure of.

Write it yourself. You can ask Codex what changed in the repo today to jog your memory, but the Decided line has to be yours, in a sentence you could defend at critique.

02

Where it lives

The log sits in the same folder as your system, so you write it where you work.

your-identity/ ├── README.md ├── AGENTS.md ├── log.md one file, newest day at the bottom ├── log/ │ ├── day-01.png │ └── day-07.png ├── src/ └── outputs/

Add the day's entry during pin-up, then say "push" to Codex. It saves with the message log: day 07, so the history shows when each entry was written.

03

Handing it in

Nobody checks your log day by day. It stays in your repository, and it's handed in with each submission: Days 1–5 with A1, Days 6–10 with A2, the whole month with A3.

  1. Write it on the day.

    Before you leave, while the day is fresh. If you miss one, write it the next morning, dated for the day it describes. A week written in one sitting is worth less to you, and it shows.

  2. Push it.

    The log goes online with everything else when you say "push", so the history shows when each entry was written.

  3. Hand it in.

    Nothing extra to do: assessment reads log.md and log/ in your repository, along with the rest of the submission.

The log is for you first: it's the record of what you decided and why, and the raw material for your process notes and reflection.

04

At critique

On critique Fridays, bring the week's five entries printed on one A4 sheet, with the images small, and pin it beside your work.

  • Critique 1 · Day 6: Days 1–5. How the identity was found.
  • Critique 2 · Day 10: Days 6–10. How the system came to speak in that voice.
  • Critique 3 · Day 15: Days 11–15. What the material taught the system.

Reviewers read the sheet before you present, so your three minutes can go on the work itself.

08

Photographing the work

Day 18, before installation. These photographs are how the work will be seen after the show closes.

Light

Even and soft

  • Daylight from a window, or two lights at about 45° to the work.
  • No direct sun, no flash straight on.
  • Gloss prints and resin: move the lights until the reflections leave the surface.
Camera

Square and steady

  • Phone or camera on a tripod or a stack of books.
  • Lens parallel to the work, centred, so edges stay straight.
  • A grey card or white paper in one frame, to check colour.
Shot list
01The whole work, straight onNothing else in the frame; crop to the edge of the paper or object.
02–04Three detailsClose enough to see the line, the layer or the texture.
05With a scaleA hand or a ruler beside it, so size reads.
06In the zoneAfter installation, with the card, manifesto and QR code.
07ProcessThe machine running, a test beside the final.
08Resin onlyThree angles, against a plain background.

Name files by shot: docs/photos/01_whole.jpg, 02_detail.jpg… Keep them under about 5 MB each in the repository; store originals on the studio drive.

Authorship reflection

Day 18. About 300 words, written by you, on what working with a machine did to your authorship.

reflection.md
# Authorship reflection

## Where Codex helped
[A specific moment, with the date from authorship.md.]

## Where it pulled toward the generic
[What it suggested that would have made the work anyone's.]

## A decision I made against it
[What you chose instead, and the identity's reason.]

## What I can now explain
[A part of the system you couldn't have explained on Day 7 and can now.]

## What I'd keep, and what I'd change, next time

This is the one document in the repository that is about you rather than the identity. Write it in your own voice.
