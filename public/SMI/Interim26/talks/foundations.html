02 · Coordinates

(0, 0) is the top-left.

x goes across, y goes down: the opposite of school graphs. Screens count pixels; plotters count millimetres.

Write positions as fractions: half the width is always the middle, on a phone or on A2.

03 · Pixels and vectors

A grid of dots, or a set of instructions.

Enlarge a raster and the squares grow. Enlarge a vector and it redraws, sharp. Click to zoom in.

Which machine wants which

Vector · SVG

The pen plotter

Follows paths. Lines only: no fills, no gradients. Tone comes from density.

Raster · PNG, TIFF

The archival printer

Lays down ink dot by dot. Tone, colour, millions of marks.

Keep your system's output as vectors; render pixels from them when you need a print. Your press does both.

04 · Resolution

A pixel has no size until you print it.

PPI: pixels per inch of paper. Prints need about 300 to look sharp up close.

A2 at 300 PPI = 4961 × 7016

About 35 megapixels. A laptop screen shows 2 to 5. Never enlarge a screenshot: render the print at full size.

05 · Colour

Light adds. Ink takes away.

A screen mixes red, green and blue light; all three is white. Paper absorbs light; more ink is darker. Some screen colours can't be printed at all.

Ask

  1. Does your identity's work want lines, or tone?
  2. Which of its colours would survive the move from screen to paper?

06 · CPU and GPU

A few strong workers, or thousands of simple ones.

The CPU does tasks one after another, very fast. The GPU does the same small task everywhere at once: exactly what millions of pixels need.

CPU

a few cores · in order

reading data, following rules, building paths

GPU

thousands of cores · all at once

shaders, live animation, huge images, 3D

07 · Rendering

While you work

Real-time

Fast enough to watch, at screen size. Each frame has about 16.7 milliseconds at 60 frames a second.

For the machine

Offline

Drawn once, as long as it takes, at full size: the A2 SVG, the 300 PPI TIFF, the STL.

Slow isn't broken. Preview small; export big. The same seed must give the same work in both.

08 · Chance and seeds

Computers can't really be random. That's the point.
