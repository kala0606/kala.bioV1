Computationally Yours.

Introduction

The theme

Out of one's place

The theme "Out of One's Place" addresses societal shifts, ecological fragility and encourages practices that foster difference, adaptation, and deep conviction in identity and freedom of expression.

Twenty days, four weeks

The plan

Week 1 · 23–27 NovIdentityTake a position. One authored identity and its manifesto.
Week 2 · 30 Nov–4 DecComputationWrite the rules. A working system and twenty outputs.
Week 3 · 7–11 DecMaterialGive it a body: plot, resin or print.
Week 4 · 14–18 DecExhibitionStand behind it. An Atlas of Elsewhere opens on Fri 18 Dec.

Three pillars

A

Displacement

Leave your familiar coordinates of place, time, language, role or species, and use the distance to see a real condition more clearly.

B

Identity

An authored identity with a name, an origin, a manifesto, beliefs and rules. A serious authorial position, not a costume.

C

Computation

An original system: parameters, rules, constraints and controlled variation, with many outputs in one recognisable language.

Masters 1 of 6 · Displacement · Ancient Courses of Fictional Rivers

A river that moved, and left every old bed showing.

Each map simulates a river as it meanders across its plain. The courses it abandons stay on the page; roads and towns gather on the banks. Hodgin drew on Harold Fisk's 1944 maps of the Mississippi.

The rule, in our words

Let the river wander. Keep every course it leaves.

Masters 2 of 6 · Displacement · Sudfah

A line sets out to say one thing.

Sudfah is Arabic for happy accident. One calligraphic line crosses the page with a story to tell; digital liquid spills on it and carries the ink somewhere the line never meant to go. The accident tells the better story.

The rule, in our words

Draw one line across. Then spill water on it.

#765 · Art Blocks Curated · CC BY-NC 4.0

Masters 3 of 6 · Identity · Memories of Qilin

Forms that almost become a creature.

The movement of classical Chinese brushwork, with the colours and patterns of ukiyo-e woodblock prints. Shapes come near dragons, phoenixes, flowers and mountains without settling. The qilin is a chimera of East Asian myth; Xie's mother saw it in the work.

The rule, in our words

Fill moving forms with inherited pattern. Stop before they become a picture.

Emily Xie, Memories of Qilin #7; #61, #223, #549 · Art Blocks · CC BY-NC 4.0

#60 · Art Blocks Curated · CC BY-NC 4.0

Masters 4 of 6 · Identity · Gazers

A self-portrait kept as a lunar calendar.

Each work begins from an Origin Moon: the date of a new moon that shaped Kane's path as an artist. From there it keeps time with the real moon. Every new moon brings a new design, and the motion speeds up for as long as the work exists.

The rule, in our words

Begin from a date in a life. Change with every new moon.

Matt Kane, Gazers #272; #14, #325, #696 · Art Blocks · CC BY-NC 4.0

Masters 5 of 6 · Computation · Fidenza

One program, from calm stripes to storms.

Structured curves and blocks, laid along a flow field: an invisible grid of directions. Scale, turbulence, density, colour and collision are dials, and each combination opens another family of pictures that are still plainly Fidenza.

The rule, in our words

Follow the field. Mind your distance from your neighbours.

Masters 6 of 6 · Computation · Ringers

A string, some pegs, no end.

Peg count, size, layout and the direction of the wrap change; the string and the pegs never do. Cherniak calls automation an artistic medium: the variety has to come from the system.

Place some pegs. Wrap a string.
