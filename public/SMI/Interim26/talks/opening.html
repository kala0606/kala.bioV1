A short history

Rules have always made art. The computer only made them faster.

In every era, ask one question: where is the author?

Before computers · South India, every morning

Kolam: a drawing you execute.

A grid of dots and a line that loops around them by rule, often without lifting. One of the oldest algorithmic drawing traditions still alive.

1790s · Musikalisches Würfelspiel

Throw two dice, sixteen times.

176 pre-written bars; each throw picks one. About 45 quadrillion possible waltzes, attributed to Mozart, though that's disputed.

The possibility space is the work.

Chance and constraint enter the studio

1913–14

Duchamp

Three Standard Stoppages: a metre of thread, dropped, and kept as it fell.

1961

Queneau

A hundred thousand billion poems: ten sonnets on strips, every line combinable.

1959–61

Drawing machines

Tinguely's Méta-Matics scribbled for visitors; Desmond Paul Henry drew with a bombsight computer.

Already the modern question: who is the artist?

1953 · before there was a computer to draw with

Ben Laposky's Oscillons.

Sine waves on an oscilloscope, photographed, exhibited as "Electronic Abstractions". The first images made electronically and shown as art.

1965 · three N's, three exhibitions, one year

FebStuttgart. Georg Nees shows plotter drawings, invited by Max Bense.
AprNew York. A. Michael Noll and Béla Julesz, Howard Wise Gallery.
NovStuttgart. Nees and Frieder Nake, Galerie Wendelin Niedlich.

They drew with pen plotters: the same kind of machine you'll use in Week 3.

A painter asked Nees if his machine could make art like his. The story goes:

"No, but it can make what you make."
Georg Nees · Stuttgart, 1965 · as told

1964 · A. Michael Noll

A program answers Mondrian.

Computer Composition with Lines, after Mondrian's Composition with Lines (1917). Shown both, most of the people Noll asked preferred the computer's.

This handbook's grid borrows from Mondrian too. Where is the author now?
