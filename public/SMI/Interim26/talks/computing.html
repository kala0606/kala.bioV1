What is computing?

From sand to sites: the machine under your identity's rules.

Before we start

Point at every computer in this room.

Phones, the projector, the fan's regulator, the lift, the card reader, your watch. Most computers don't look like computers.

A definition to argue with

Computing is changing symbols by rules.

Until the 1950s, "computer" was a job title: people, very often women, who calculated by hand from written procedures. The machine took the name from them.

The tower

Eleven simple ideas, stacked.

Each floor understands only the floor below. Your sketch sits at the very top.

Out of one's place

Every floor keeps a small promise and hides how.

That's abstraction. A web page doesn't know about transistors. This month you'll do something similar: speak from one floor of the world while knowing what's underneath.

Floor 01 · bits

Everything is switches.

Eight switches make a byte: 256 patterns. Give each column a value, doubling right to left, and add the ones that are on.

Meaning is an agreement

A colour is three bytes.

Red, green and blue light, each 0 to 255. A screen adds light; paint takes it away.

Two minutes, on paper

Write your initials in binary.

A = 65, B = 66 … Z = 90. Then read them as a colour: what colour is your name?

Floor 02 · transistors

A switch made of sand.

Silicon, doped. A voltage on the gate opens a channel; take it away and the channel closes. The output of one switch can flip the next.

How small, how many

1945ENIAC: 17,468 vacuum tubes. Fills a room.
1947The transistor, Bell Labs. Thumb-sized.
1958–59The integrated circuit: many on one chip.
1971Intel 4004: about 2,300 transistors.
TodayTens of billions on a laptop chip, each a few dozen nanometres.

Chips are made by photolithography: light through a stencil, etched in layers. A chip is a print. Printmaking, at the scale of atoms.

Floor 03 · gates

Switches that decide.

In a row: both must be on (AND). Side by side: either will do (OR). Boole wrote the algebra in 1854; Shannon saw circuits obey it in 1937.

Ask

Every if you write is a gate.

  1. What is the first if your identity would ask of the world?
  2. What would it refuse with a not?

Floor 04 · adders

Arithmetic out of logic.

Add two bits and there are four cases. The sum is XOR; the carry is AND. Chain 64 of these and you have the adder in your laptop.

Floors 05–06 · memory, clock, CPU

Fetch, decode, execute. Forever.

Instructions live in the same memory as data (von Neumann, 1945). A clock ticks about three billion times a second, and everything steps together.

Floors 07–08 · the machine and the OS

Far is slow, so everything caches.

One trip to memory costs hundreds of ticks of waiting. The operating system lets programs take turns so fast it looks simultaneous.

For you: your whole draw() has about 16.7 milliseconds per frame at 60 fps.

Floors 09–10 · the network, the browser

What happens when you press Enter.

The leaks

The layers leak.

  • 255 + 1 can give 0: a byte has run out of switches.
  • A loop over pixels is slow; a shader is fast: CPU against GPU.
  • Your sketch looks different on a phone: a different floor 07.
  • Glitch art, data bending, net art: artists working the leaks on purpose.
