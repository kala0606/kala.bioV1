The idea is the machine.

The certificate

He never drew most of his wall drawings. He wrote them.

From 1968, more than twelve hundred. A museum buys a certificate and a diagram, not a wall. Painted over, drawn again elsewhere by other hands: still the same work.

Who is the author?

Wall Drawing #118, 1971 · paraphrased

Fifty points at random, evenly spread. Connect every point to every other point with a straight line.

Drafter A · pure random
Drafter B · kept apart

Drawn live: new points every time this slide opens. Which one did LeWitt mean? We don't know. Neither did his drafters.

The idea that matters

Left free

Be the drafter · four minutes

Draw ten straight lines, each starting at an edge of the paper, none touching another.

Then hold them up. Same instruction, thirteen drawings. Which words were clear, and which only felt clear?

4:00

Vera Molnár · machine imaginaire

From 1959 she ran programs by hand.

Simple procedures, executed step by step, as if she were the machine. When she reached a real computer in 1968, she already thought like one.

The vocabulary of an instruction

Space

where?

"on a wall", "a 6-foot square"

Element

what mark?

"straight lines", "arcs", "broken lines"

Placement

from where to where?

"from the corners", "to points on a grid"

Quantity

how many?

"fifty", "as many as possible"

Constraint

what's forbidden?

"not touching", "within the circle"

System

how do parts relate?

"all combinations", "superimposed"

All combinations · after Four Basic Kinds of Straight Lines, 1969

Refuse to choose.

Four kinds of line, every combination: fifteen squares. Count 1 to 15 in binary and read the four bits as four switches.

Yesterday's switches, today's composition.

Why it matters

Permutation is a way to escape your own taste.

The system forces you to look at combinations you'd never pick. Some of them will be the best things in the series.

Words into code

The wordsOne possible codeThe decision you just made
"fifty points"for i in 0..50a constant, or a dial?
"at random"random(width)every place equally likely
"evenly spread"a jittered gridwhich kind of even?
"not touching"try, check, throw awaya machine's way, not a hand's

The verbs are easy. The adjectives are where the art goes.

Where rules come from · six questions

Permutation

What are all the ways to combine my parts?

Accumulation

What if each mark depends on the last?

Recursion

What if the rule applies to its own output?

Constraint

What may never happen?

Translation

What data from my world could become form?

Chance

Where do I stop deciding?

Steal structure, not style

The richest rules are already lying around.

  • Kolam: a grid of dots, one looping line, every morning.
  • Weaving: over or under; Jacquard's cards, 1804.
  • Games: kabaddi, carrom, chess: constraints on movement.
  • Scores: Yoko Ono's Grapefruit, 1964.
  • Bureaucracy: forms, queues, timetables.

Back to this month

Your identity writes the certificate. The computer is the drafter.

Your identity's voice is authored. Like LeWitt's walls, the hand is delegated. What stays yours is the instruction, and every word in it.
