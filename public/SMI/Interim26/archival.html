01

Why a print

An archival print uses pigment inks on archival paper, made to last. It can hold what a pen can't: tone, colour, fine density and millions of marks.

Choose it because the identity's work needs tone or colour, not only because it's quick. If you're printing as a fallback for a plot or a resin piece, prepare the file early so it's ready if needed. Framing and mounting are still being arranged. To be updated

02

The file

Render at full resolution from your system. Never enlarge a small image.

Final sizePixels at 300 PPI
A4 · 210 × 297 mm2480 × 3508
A3 · 297 × 420 mm3508 × 4961
A2 · 420 × 594 mm4961 × 7016
  • Format: TIFF, flattened, with the colour profile embedded.
  • Colour: work and export in the profile the facilitator gives you for the printer and paper. Soft-proof on screen before printing.
  • Vector work: an SVG can be rendered straight to 300 PPI; ask Codex to rasterise it at the exact pixel size (prompt 18 in the library).
  • Margins: leave a border of plain paper unless you've agreed a borderless print.

Maximum print width depends on the printer. Confirm on Day 11

03

Paper

Matte cotton rag

Soft and deep

No glare, a soft surface, rich tone. Blacks are less dense; very fine detail softens slightly.

Gloss or baryta

Sharp and dense

Deep blacks and crisp detail, with some sheen. Shows fingerprints and reflects light.

The paper is part of the identity's position. A record of loss on a glossy sheet reads differently from the same image on matte rag. Say why in your spec sheet.

04

Test strips and proofs

Days 12–13. Print before you print.

  1. Test strip.

    A narrow strip across the darkest, lightest and finest parts of the image, at full size, on the final paper.

  2. Look in daylight and under the exhibition light.

    Do shadows block up? Do fine lines hold? Is the colour what you saw on screen?

  3. Adjust in the system, not by hand.

    Change parameters or the colour mapping and re-render, so the final is still the system's output.

  4. Full proof, then final.

    A full-size proof once the strip is right; the final on Days 16–17.

05

Handling

  • Clean, dry hands, or cotton gloves. Hold prints by the edges.
  • Let it dry. Leave prints flat and uncovered for a day before stacking, sleeving or framing.
  • Store flat, with tissue between prints, away from sunlight and damp.

07

When it goes wrong

ProblemLikely cause and fix
Soft or jagged edgesThe image was upscaled. Render again at the full pixel size.
Colours duller than on screenWrong profile, or an over-bright screen. Soft-proof with the paper's profile and adjust in the system.
Shadows are a flat blackDark tones too close together. Lift the darkest values slightly and test again.
Scuffs or marksHandled before it dried. Reprint, and leave it flat for a day.
